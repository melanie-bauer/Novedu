= LiteLLM Budget Validation
== Budget per Student (OpenWebUI + Azure)
:author: Novedu
:toc:
:toc-title: Table of Contents

== Evaluation Criteria

* Budget Enforcement
- The per-student budget must not only be tracked, but also reliably blocked once exceeded.
* Integration (OpenWebUI → LiteLLM)
- Student identity must arrive in the gateway reproducibly as a Customer/End-User..
* Operational Behavior
- Reset logic (e.g., daily) and cost/spend calculation must be transparent and verifiable.
* Data Protection
- Store only usage/spend; no prompt/response logging in plaintext.

== 1. Case: Header-based student identity is not mapped correctly (tracking incorrect or missing)

=== Description
OpenWebUI can forward user information via headers (e.g., user email/id/name).
If LiteLLM does not read/map these headers correctly, the student identity is either:

- not set at all, or
- mapped to an incorrect/constant default.

In that case, all “per-student” budgets become ineffective because requests are not attributed to the correct identity.

=== Typical Symptoms
- No (or wrong) customer IDs appear in the LiteLLM UI.
- Spend logs show only one identity (e.g., `default_user_id`) for all students.
- Filtering by customer/end-user returns no results.

=== What to Verify
- Does LiteLLM receive a unique `user/end_user/customer` identifier per request?

=== Findings
Using the Swagger UI, two identical POST requests were sent with `user: "student1@schule.at"` and `user: "student2@schule.at"`.
Both requests were answered successfully with 200 OK. In `spend/logs` and `/customer/daily/activity`, the requests were correctly separated and recorded as `end_user`.
Therefore, students can be clearly distinguished for tracking.

== 2. Case: End-user budget is not enforced (tracking only, no blocking)

=== Description
There are bug reports where end-user budgets track spend, but requests are not rejected after the budget is exceeded.

=== Why this Matters
This is a risk scenario: “budget per student” would be reporting only, without real cost control.

=== Typical Symptoms
- Customer/end-user spend increases beyond `max_budget`.
- Requests continue to succeed (no 4xx/429/403 error).

=== What to Verify
- Clear error behavior when the budget is exceeded (status code + message).
- No further provider call once the budget is exceeded.

=== Findings
For end-user `budgettest1@schule.at`, a very small budget was set (`max_budget=0.00001`).
After a first request with high token output, spend increased and exceeded the budget. The following request was blocked by LiteLLM and returned an `ExceededBudget (type: budget_exceeded)` error.
This proves that, in this deployment, LiteLLM not only tracks per-student budget limits, but enforces them reliably. More issues follow.

== 3. Case: Budget reset / “per day” (timezone / rolling window) -> thorougly tested

=== Description
The critical question is when LiteLLM resets a budget window (rolling window vs. calendar day / timezone).
In the tests, an end-user with a very small `max_budget` and a short `budget_duration` (e.g., `60s`) was pushed over budget.
Afterwards, it was repeatedly checked whether access would be re-enabled once the duration had elapsed.

Result: budget enforcement (`ExceededBudget`) works reliably, but the reset is not visible exactly after the configured duration and may appear different depending on the endpoint (asynchronous reporting / caching).

...asynchronous reporting => LiteLLM decides immediately on incoming requests (budget gate: allow/block), but values exposed through reporting endpoints are partially aggregated views and/or updated with a delay.

=== Typical Symptoms
- `ExceededBudget` occurs even though `spend: 0` is shown in `customer/info` or `customer/list`.
- Reset happens delayed / “jumpy” (not exactly after e.g. 60s); afterwards, requests may work again.

=== What to Verify
- Is access actually re-enabled after `budget_duration` expires (reproducibly), or are there delayed/asynchronous resets?
- Are budget decisions at `chat/completions` consistent with reporting (`customer/*`, `spend/logs`)?
- For `budget_duration="1d"`: verify rolling 24h vs. calendar-day reset (UTC vs. local timezone) across a day change and via daily-activity endpoints.

=== Findings
During testing with `resettest3@schule.at` (`max_budget=0.00001`, `budget_duration="60s"`), the budget was exceeded and LiteLLM successfully blocked further calls with `ExceededBudget`.
During observation, `customer/*` endpoints sometimes showed `spend: 0` even though requests were still blocked. After a few additional requests, the budget was exceeded again (e.g., `Spend=1.419e-05 > Budget=1e-05`).
This indicates delayed/asynchronous resetting or differing data states between the budget gate and reporting.
For the “per-day” scenario, the reset timing (rolling window vs. calendar day / timezone implementation) must be verified, tested, and documented in more depth.

== 4. Conclusion
Based on the performed prognosis LiteLLM could be a viable Choise, however it still needs extensive Testing and maybe even extensions to reach a truly acceptable state. LiteLLM already can distinguish between students and block users from exceeding the budget, once they breach their limit.

The main risk however is the reset behaviour (explained in Case 3): budget rests were not consistently observed at the time of their configured window, and reporting endpoints sometimes showed values that did not match the immediate budget-gate behaviour. Therefore additional Tests over multiple days, maybe even a week are required (UTC vs local timezones) and under realistic loads (multiple students in parallel), in order that predictable "per-day" behaviour and reporting consistency and be confirmed.

If daily reset behaviour cannot be proven reliable, then an additional operational safeguard (e.g a seperate reset / pre-call budget guard) should be considered to ensure strict cost control.
